Why AI gets your numbers wrong

Two teams. Two definitions of discount.

AI tools write good SQL now. What they can't see is your business: the Slack thread where finance and growth agreed what discount means. So the AI picks one, and nobody notices.

  • Definitions

    The same word means two numbers

    Discount, active user, churn. Each team has its own formula, a few percent apart on every row.

  • Known scars

    Your data has history

    A week of missing refunds. A status change halfway through a quarter. Your analysts route around these from memory. A model can't.

  • Schema traps

    Joins fail quietly

    IDs stored as text in one table and numbers in another. UTC timestamps in an IST business. The totals still look plausible.

What we build

A context engine, in plain files you own.

We write down what your best analyst knows and connect it to the AI your team already uses. It lives in your git repository as Markdown and Python, so there's nothing to migrate, and it stays yours after we leave.

  • metrics/ decisions/

    Context notes

    One note per metric, decision and data trap, each with an owner and a date. Old definitions are marked superseded, never deleted.

  • CLAUDE.md

    Rules for the AI

    Ask when two definitions conflict. Never mix internal and investor numbers. Nothing ships without a person signing off.

  • lib/

    A tested query layer

    Joins, time zones and filters written once, so every answer starts from the same base tables.

  • checks

    Upkeep

    Checks catch stale notes, broken links and source tables that stopped updating, before a wrong number goes out.

Questions founders ask

Before you book

Why only one company at a time?

Because the founders do the work. Both of us are on your project for the full six weeks, with no junior team behind the curtain and no other client competing for our attention.

What does it cost?

One fixed price for the six weeks, agreed after your free accuracy score and before any work starts. Half up front, half when 27 of your 30 questions come back right.

What happens after the six weeks?

You own everything. It lives in your repository, your area owners know how to keep it current, and you can re-run your question set any time. If you'd like, there's an optional quarterly tune-up.

Do you need access to our data?

Read-only access to your warehouse during the project, with a per-person login you can revoke. We never need write access, and the context notes hold definitions, not customer data.

Which warehouses do you work with?

We've run this in production on BigQuery, Postgres, MySQL and Google Sheets. Snowflake and Databricks work the same way; we confirm the details before we start.

We already bought an AI analytics tool. Do we still need this?

Probably more than before. Tools like these write good SQL, but they don't know your definitions or your data's history. We write that down where your tool can read it. Keep the tool you like.

We already have a semantic layer. Do we need this?

A semantic layer defines how a metric is computed. It doesn't record why it changed, which data to distrust or what to do when two teams disagree. We build on top of yours.
